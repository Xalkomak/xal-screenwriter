# Stage Play

## Overview

The Stage Play document type is intended for writing theatrical scripts
intended for live performance.

A stage play is organized around theatrical structure, spoken dialogue,
characters, and stage directions.

The document type does not prescribe a single universal visual style for
stage plays. Individual playwrights and publishers may use different
layouts and conventions.

## Elements

### Act

A major structural division of a play.

Example:

ACT ONE

### Scene

A subdivision within an act.

Example:

SCENE ONE

### Stage Direction

Text describing actions, movement, setting, entrances, exits, lighting,
sound, or other information intended for the production.

### Character

Identifies the character who is about to speak.

Character names are displayed in uppercase.

Example:

PENELOPE

### Parenthetical

A short direction associated with a character's speech.

Example:

(cautiously)

### Dialogue

Spoken text belonging to the preceding character.

Example:

I don't think we're alone.

## Notes

Stage plays do not necessarily follow the same structural or formatting
conventions as screenplays.

The document type therefore treats acts, scenes, stage directions,
characters, parentheticals, and dialogue as semantic elements rather than
requiring screenplay-specific notation.
